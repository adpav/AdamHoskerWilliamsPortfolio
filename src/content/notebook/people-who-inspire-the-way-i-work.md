There are certain people I keep coming back to. People whose work or words resonate with me and often provide a bit of inspiration.

I started my career as a graphic designer, and I often try to remember the spark I had back then. How much I wanted to learn and grow, and what I hoped I might achieve.

Those thoughts can sometimes feel a long way off in the day-to-day of work. Things can become a bit paint-by-numbers, and it can sometimes feel like there's little room for drawing outside the lines.

But taking in some of what the people below share reminds me of that earlier enthusiasm. It makes me feel a bit like my younger self again, and a little less jaded!

## Andy Clarke

Andy is probably one of my biggest inspirations. He's always talking about making memorable designs, and that's something that comes through in all of his work.

His articles and videos are consistently engaging, interesting and inspiring. I'm often reminded not to settle for mediocre and to push creative boundaries when I read or watch his work.

Even the things he shares about layout design are worth paying attention to. I always feel like I've learned something from seeing how he approaches and puts layouts together.

[Stuff & Nonsense](https://stuffandnonsense.co.uk/)  |  [YouTube series](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iIHHjYksvZU)

## Mike Kus and Dann Petty

Mike Kus and Dann Petty are two designers whose work I find really inspiring. There's a freshness and energy to what they create, and I love the combination of graphic design, typography, photography and digital design.

I find there's a lot to learn just from looking at their work. Seeing the ideas they've explored, how they've approached a particular layout or used imagery and typography. It's the sort of work that makes me think about what I might try myself when faced with a similar challenge.

I don't know either of them as well as I do Andy Clarke, in terms of their wider thinking and writing, but their creative work speaks for itself.

[Mike Kus](https://mikekus.com/)  |  [Dann Petty](https://www.dannpetty.com/)

## Brad Frost and Dan Mall

Brad Frost's Atomic Design was one of those ideas that instantly clicked with me. The idea of starting small and building up, thinking about how individual components fit together to form a wider system, really shaped some of my early thoughts around design systems.

I also really like Dan Mall's design work, but it's his talks and conversations with Brad about design systems that I find particularly interesting. Their 'hot potato-ing' approach to working together is great to watch. Trying things, bouncing ideas around, responding to each other's thinking and iterating together. It's a way of working that really appeals to me, and reflects how I like to work with developers myself.

[Atomic Design](https://atomicdesign.bradfrost.com/)  |  [The Hot Potato Process](https://v5.danmall.com/posts/hot-potato-process/)  |  [Dan Mall Design Battle](https://www.youtube.com/live/g1wgj-pP7yc?si=GIrp3hj_hJdP6V8N)

## Paul Boag

I've always liked and resonated with what Paul Boag writes about UX, UI and strategy. His content feels practical and grounded in the realities of day-to-day work, rather than just how things should work in an ideal world.

I particularly like his series with Balsamiq on UI design and psychology. It's a great example of his knowledge and approach to design, taking you through the thinking behind good user experiences and how to apply it in practice.

It's the sort of content I find myself coming back to, particularly when the work I'm doing is more focused on UX and strategy than visual design.

[Paul Boag's Balsamiq learning track: UI Design Psychology](https://balsamiq.com/learn/learning-tracks/ui-design-psychology/)

## CSS folks

Andy Bell, Ahmed CSS, Kevin Powell and Rachel Andrew have all helped me get to grips with the more technical side of front-end development.

I particularly like Andy's approach to leaning into what the web platform can do, rather than reaching for JavaScript or more complicated solutions when the browser already has the tools to do the job.

Ahmed and Kevin are great for their CSS tips and techniques, while Rachel Andrew has been a go-to for learning about CSS Grid and Flexbox.

What I really like about all of them is how they explain technical things in an easy-to-understand way. They've been a bit of a gateway into a more technical world for me, helping me understand what's possible and encouraging me to explore things further.

[Andy Bell's Piccalilli](https://piccalil.li/) | [Ahmad Shadeed](https://ishadeed.com/)| [Kevin Powell](https://www.kevinpowell.co/)| [Rachel Andrew](https://rachelandrew.co.uk/)

## Tobias van Schneider

I enjoy Tobias's writing and the different perspectives he brings to design and creativity. He often talks about looking outside of design for inspiration, which is something I think is easy to forget when you're immersed in the industry.

His short articles and notes are often thought-provoking and offer a refreshing perspective on things. I like his design work too, but it's his writing and ideas that I find myself coming back to most.

[House of van Schneider](https://vanschneider.com/) | [DESK blog](https://vanschneider.com/blog/)

---

These are some of the people who help keep my enthusiasm for good design alive, especially when the day-to-day of enterprise work can sometimes get in the way.
